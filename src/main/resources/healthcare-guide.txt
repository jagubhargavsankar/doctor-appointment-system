
HEALTHCARE EDUCATION GUIDE

1. Skin Allergies

Skin allergies can cause itching, redness, rashes, swelling, or hives.
Common triggers include certain metals, fragrances, cosmetics, latex,
some medicines, and insect bites. The cause cannot always be determined
from symptoms alone.

A dermatologist can evaluate persistent or recurring skin problems.
Seek urgent medical attention for a severe allergic reaction, especially
if there is difficulty breathing or swelling of the tongue or throat.

2. Choosing the Right Doctor

A dermatologist specializes in conditions involving the skin, hair,
and nails.

A dentist helps with teeth, gums, and oral health.

A cardiologist specializes in conditions affecting the heart.

An orthopedic specialist evaluates conditions affecting bones,
joints, muscles, and related structures.

A general physician can assess many common health concerns and help
determine whether referral to a specialist is needed.

The appropriate specialist depends on the person's symptoms and
medical circumstances. A document-based chatbot should not replace
a professional medical evaluation.

3. General Health Information

Fever, cough, headache, and pain can have many possible causes.
A chatbot cannot establish a diagnosis from these symptoms alone.

People with persistent, worsening, or concerning symptoms should seek
advice from a qualified healthcare professional.

For severe symptoms or a suspected medical emergency, seek emergency
medical care rather than relying on an AI response.

4. Using Healthcare Documents in an AI Chatbot

Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, allows an AI application to
search relevant passages from a supplied document before generating
an answer.

The document is divided into smaller chunks. An embedding model converts
each chunk into a numerical representation. A vector store keeps those
representations and their associated text.

When a user asks a question, the application searches for relevant
chunks and provides their content to the AI model as context.
Demo reference code for the skin-allergy section: SKIN-7314.
The AI should answer from the retrieved information, distinguish
missing information from documented facts, and avoid inventing details.

5. Preparing for a Patient Visit

Before a routine healthcare visit, write down the main reason for the visit,
when symptoms started, what makes them better or worse, and any questions you
want to ask. Bring a list of current medicines and supplements, known allergies,
relevant medical history, and previous test results if available. Bring the
identification, insurance information, or referral documents requested by your
clinic. Arrive at the time recommended by the clinic. Clinic requirements can
vary, so contact the clinic if you are unsure what documents to bring.

6. Understanding Healthcare Terms

A healthcare term is a word used to describe a body part, symptom, test,
condition, treatment, or healthcare process. Ask for the specific word or phrase
when you want a definition. An explanation should describe the term in plain
language and should not be treated as a personal diagnosis. The meaning of a
term can depend on the context in which a clinician uses it. Ask your healthcare
professional to explain how a term applies to your own care.

7. Questions for Your Clinician

Useful questions include: What does this term mean? What are the next steps?
Are there preparation instructions for the test or appointment? When should I
expect results? Who should I contact if symptoms worsen? The appropriate answer
depends on the person's circumstances and the clinic's instructions.
